DWARF — cardano-node vs Amaru Long-Range Forged-Fork Differential

Costless-simulation long-range attack: forge a real, longer alternative chain and test both implementations' chain-selection · generated 2026-07-17
The strong form of the long-range attack: instead of merely commanding a rollback, the attacker forges a genuinely valid, longer alternative chain that branches > k blocks in the past and presents it. Does an honest node adopt this better-looking forged history (a > k rollback), or refuse — and does the new Amaru node decide identically to cardano-node? Both nodes, seeded on the honest chain at block 30520 and offered the forged fork (tip 30553, forking 20 blocks back), REFUSED it. No divergence.
Forge (real crypto, logical time). Using the ouroboros-consensus-cardano tools already in the stack — db-truncater + db-synthesizer + immdb-server — copy a honest chain-db, truncate to fork point block 30500 (> k behind), then db-synthesizer --append with an honest operator's real KES/VRF/opcert credentials forges a validly-signed alternative continuation in logical slot order. This sidesteps the wall-clock/KES-exhaustion wall that defeats any live-node approach. Forged fork: tip block 30553, forking at 30500.

A validly-forged, longer alternative chain that branches > k back is rejected by both implementations.

Method

Serve via a real cardano-node relay. The forged fork is served by an isolated cardano-node (attacker-relay) seeded at the forged chain-db, topology dialing the victim back, ProtocolIdleTimeout raised to 600 s (Amaru peers cleanly with cardano-node relays; it does not sync reliably from immdb-server directly). Victims seeded in the past. A cardano-node and an Amaru node are each seeded on the honest chain at block 30520 — just past the fork — so the forged fork (30553) is genuinely longer from their view but forks > k back. Oracle: each victim must keep 30520 and adopt none of the fork's blocks; both must decide identically.

Behavioral observation (not a safety divergence)

The two nodes refuse via different mechanisms. cardano-node offers chain-sync intersect points geometrically deep, so it can latch onto a fork up to k back, evaluate it, and reject the > k rollback. Amaru offers only its ~k most-recent points on FindIntersect (blocks 30516–30520, all after the fork point), so the fork (branching 20 back) has no common point among them → intersect not found → Amaru stops and adopts nothing. Same safe outcome, but Amaru will not even engage a fork > k back — likely also the root of its finickiness syncing from non-cardano-node servers. Worth flagging to the Amaru team.

Scope caveat

This devnet runs pure Praos (LoEAndGDDDisabled, CSJDisabled), so what is proven is the Praos k-rollback bound against a real forged fork — a stronger, higher-fidelity result than the commanded-rollback test. It does not exercise Ouroboros Genesis's density-based chain-selection (which decides between deep forks by density in the genesis window). Probing that specifically requires the devnet reconfigured with LoE/GDD enabled — the follow-on.

Bottom line

The costless-simulation forging pipeline is real and working (db-truncater → db-synthesizer → cardano-node relay serve), and a genuinely-forged, longer, deep fork is rejected identically by cardano-node and Amaru. A positive consensus-equivalence result on the strongest achievable long-range stimulus under Praos.
