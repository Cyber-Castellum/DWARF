DWARF — Genesis Follow-On & Amaru Chain-Selection Finding

cardano-node (Ouroboros Genesis) vs Amaru (Praos) chain-selection on the cardano_amaru devnet · generated 2026-07-17
Across the full consensus differential suite — a 4-hour chain-selection soak (466 iterations, 0 divergences), a deep-rollback differential, and a costless-simulation forged-fork differential — cardano-node and Amaru reached the same chain-selection decision every time. No consensus-safety divergence. The Genesis-enabled follow-on surfaced two implementation differences worth the Amaru team's attention — not bugs, but real behavioural differences in chain-selection.

Finding 1 — Amaru implements Praos only; no Ouroboros Genesis

cardano-node runs Ouroboros Genesis (ConsensusMode: GenesisMode — LoE + GDD + CSJ + ChainSync LoP bucket); we enabled it on the devnet and confirmed it initialises and syncs. Amaru shows no Genesis support — no consensus-mode flag, no Genesis/GDD/density options, env, or binary symbols; its only chain-selection parameters are Praos (security-param k, active-slot-coeff, KES). different chain-selection rules As the network activates Genesis, a Praos-only node uses the older longest-chain + k-limit rule; Genesis' distinctive protection (defeating a long-range/eclipse attacker that offers a longer-but-sparser chain to a bootstrapping node) is a mainnet-scale, many-peer scenario a devnet cannot reproduce.

Finding 2 — Shallow chain-sync intersect (~k recent points)

On MsgFindIntersect, Amaru offers only its ~k most-recent chain points. Consequences observed repeatedly: (1) it cannot engage a fork that branches more than ~k blocks back — responds intersect not found and drops the peer (conservative/safe, but it will not evaluate a deep alternative); (2) it is finicky syncing from non-cardano-node servers — it synced cleanly from real cardano-node relays but failed against the consensus tool immdb-server, where cardano-node (which offers intersect points geometrically deep) had no trouble.

Why no divergence (the honest conclusion)

The k-rollback limit is shared by both implementations and both consensus modes, so it governs the deep-fork/incumbent decision regardless of Praos vs Genesis. A fork Amaru can engage (within k) is not one GDD would distinctively reject; a fork GDD would distinctively reject (deep, sparse) is one Amaru's k-limit already refuses. The two conditions contradict, so no clean deterministic divergence is constructible on a 2-peer devnet.

Recommendations

1. Confirm whether Ouroboros Genesis is on Amaru's roadmap — a Praos-only node has weaker bootstrapping/long-range protection than cardano-node once mainnet activates Genesis. 2. Review chain-sync intersect-point selection (offer points geometrically deep, as cardano-node does) — improves interop and lets Amaru evaluate (and correctly reject) deeper forks rather than dropping the peer. 3. The harness (forging pipeline + real-relay serving + differential oracle) is reusable against a Genesis-enabled, multi-peer topology to probe the density rule at scale.
