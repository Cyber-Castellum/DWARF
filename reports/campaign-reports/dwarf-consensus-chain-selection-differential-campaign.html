DWARF — cardano-node vs Amaru Chain-Selection Differential

Cross-implementation consensus differential on the upstream cardano_amaru mesh · 4-hour soak campaign · generated 2026-07-16
The central consensus question: does the Haskell cardano-node ever disagree with Amaru on which chain is canonical? A disagreement is a network split — the highest-value failure. DWARF ran a node-agnostic Common-Prefix oracle across five regimes — honest reorg, within-k partition recovery, an epoch boundary, a VRF tiebreak, and a private-fork-under-delay — looping each for 4 hours. Across 466 independent iterations (~20 cumulative hours), cardano-node and Amaru selected the identical chain. Zero genuine divergences.
Substrate. The real upstream cardano_amaru topology: 3 Haskell block producers (p1–p3, cardano-node 10.7.1) and 2 relays, alongside 2 native Amaru relays and an Amaru-fed consumer (a cardano-node that syncs through Amaru, so its tip reflects what Amaru selected). Fast profile: k=5, epoch 125 slots, active-slot-coeff 0.2, Conway at epoch 0. Amaru carries no stake — it is the convergence observer.

What each scenario establishes

chainhold — under repeated real reorgs (a producer is cut off and must roll back on reconnect), Amaru tracks the same canonical chain as cardano-node. Strongest single piece of evidence: 80 induced fork/reorg cycles, all agree.

rollback-within-k — after a short (<k) partition heals, both the honest cardano-node side and Amaru re-converge to the same chain; the partitioned producer recovers correctly.

epoch-boundary — the nonce-evolution / stake-snapshot handoff induces no disagreement across epoch transitions.

stage1-tiebreak — read directly from a native Amaru relay (not the consumer proxy), Amaru's selected chain matches cardano-node's, including through natural VRF-tiebreak competition.

threshold-delay-probe — (cardano-node-only) a private chainsync fork under bounded network delay does not break Common-Prefix consistency among honest nodes.

Method

Oracle: impl-aware Common Prefix — the cardano-node group and the Amaru group must agree on the selected tip (chain_select_differential), read hash-for-hash and time-aligned. Loop: each scenario looped 4 h with a JSONL heartbeat and bounded bundle retention (all failures kept, passing bundles pruned — no runaway logs). Counts (47–154) reflect per-iteration cost, not a cap: a chainhold cycle includes a 90 s partition + 70 s settle (~180 s → ~80 in 4 h); observe-only scenarios run more. This is a soak / repeatability validation, not a fuzzing campaign — exhaustive adversarial exploration is the Antithesis role, and adversarial-stake security testing is separate (Design Doc B).

Bottom line

The cross-implementation chain-selection oracle was exercised under honest operation, induced forks with reorg, within-k partition recovery, an epoch boundary, a VRF tiebreak, and a private-fork-under-delay attack — 466 iterations, no divergence. On the exercised regimes, cardano-node and Amaru do not disagree on which chain is real. A positive consensus-equivalence result.
